Aerodynamic Design
Overall Configuration: A conventional fixed-wing configuration was chosen, featuring a high aspect ratio wing to minimize induced drag, combined with a V-tail configuration and rear positioned propeller.
Fuselage Design: The fuselage has a streamlined, low-frontal-area profile. The integration of the underside camera turret introduced a necessary aerodynamic disruption. 
Landing Gear: A fixed, aerodynamically faired tricycle landing gear was chosen for simplicity, reliability and weight reduction. It’s unclear how much this will increase drag.
Thermal Management: to cool down the battery pack and small electronic components, a small duct was designed, which can also be covered if cold weather is expected. 

Structural Design and Materials
Primary Structure: The fuselage, wings, and tail assembly are constructed from Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer (CFRP) composites. 
High-Stress Components: Most critical moving parts, such as control surface hinges and the payload gimbal, are machined from Aluminum 7075-T6.
Propeller: 3D printed high-strength ABS, allowing for replacements to be rapidly executed on the field.
Battery Pack casing: Polyamide with rubber supports to protect from vibrations.

Propulsion Components Selection 
Motor: A Brushless DC (BLDC) Outrunner motor was chosen. Its outrunner configuration provides the high torque needed to efficiently spin a large-diameter propeller at lower RPMs, ideal for endurance.
Electronic Speed Controller (ESC): A high-performance ESC manages the motor, providing critical telemetry data, including voltage, current, and temperature.
Battery Pack: A Lithium Polymer (LiPo) battery with a 40,000 mAh capacity was designed to meet the endurance target with a 30min surplus. 

Structural Bench Testing 
During static and dynamic load testing, the airframe was subjected to its design limit loads and vibration tests, which led to several modifications. A 3 cm fuselage crack found at 95% of the limit load necessitated a 300g structural reinforcement. The 3D-printed propeller showed fatigue cracks and was replaced with a more durable carbon fiber version. Finally, the battery cooling ducts were redesigned to simplify manufacturing.

Wind Tunnel Testing
An aerodynamics campaign using a scale model validated the CFD predictions, confirming the aircraft's handling was within expectations, though the landing gear showed more flow disturbance than simulated.
Separately, the original cooling system (before structural changes) was tested with the full-scale propulsion system at 30°C. While the motor performed as expected, a thermal issue was identified: during a 1-hour simulated cruise, the battery's temperature rose to 45°C, 25% higher than predicted.

Propulsion System Bench Testing
During the electric propulsion system testing it was observed that the carbon fiber propeller produced about 3% less static thrust than a 3D printed version at the same power. At the predicted cruise thrust setting, the system drew approximately 460W at 6000 rpm, resulting in an estimated endurance of 3 hours and 10 minutes.

Flight Testing
The team could only do one testing session, spending a battery worth of testing, in the following conditions:
Ambient temperature at ground level: ~ 20 °CS
Wind: Up to 5m/s, no precipitation and no clouds.
Payload: electro-Optical/Infrared (EO/IR) camera turret weighting 1.85kg. 
Aircraft Specs Comparison: Empty weight - Design Target: 17.96 Kg, Measured: 18.45 Kg (increase of 0.49 Kg or 2.7%) 
Gross weight - Measured: 20.30 Kg 
Length - Design Target: 1.85m, Measured: 1.85m (no change) 
Wingspan - Design Target: 3.42m, Measured: 3.43m (increase of 0.01m)

The flight plan integrated multiple test objectives, with 4 steps:
Run 1: Handling Checks (20 min)
The UAV showed predictable and responsive handling. A slight left yaw was observed, which was traced to a minor wing defect and corrected before the next run. All systems and temperatures were nominal.
Run 2: Maximum Speed Validation (10 min)
The aircraft achieved its predicted maximum speed and remained stable. However, the pilot noted the throttle response became slightly sluggish on the final high-speed pass. Telemetry confirmed spikes in power draw, and battery and motor temperatures rose and were slower to return to baseline than expected.
Run 3: Climb/Descent Assessment (15 min)
Initial climb performance was strong and matched predictions. However, subsequent climbs felt less energetic and required noticeably more throttle input to achieve the same ascent rate, suggesting a decrease in system efficiency.
Run 4: Endurance Cruise (95 min)
Significant performance degradation was observed during the long-duration cruise. After about 50 minutes, the pilot reported the UAV required progressively more throttle to maintain speed. Upon landing, the battery casing and avionics bay were significantly hotter than anticipated, confirming telemetry data that showed a sustained rise in component temperatures and a clear degradation in system efficiency.

This high cruise power consumption is the direct cause of the rapid battery depletion (the slope of the SoC line) and, consequently, the failure to meet the endurance target.

Graph: Ambient, Motor and Battery Temperature over Flight Time during Run 4

Y-Axis: "Temperature ($\text{^oC}$)", with a scale from 0 to 140.
X-Axis: "Flight Time (minutes)".
This graph's data is critical and shows a severe thermal management problem, which correlates directly with the power and flight profile graphs:
1.Ground & Initial Climb (Time 0 to 4 minutes):
Ambient (Light Blue): Starts at a ground temperature of 25C and drops quickly to 12C as the UAV climbs.
Motor (Red): Rises extremely rapidly from the ambient 25C to over 90C, driven by the high power draw of the climb.
Battery (Green): Also rises sharply, from 25C to 52C.
2.First Cruise (Time 4 to 40 minutes):
Ambient (Light Blue): Stays constant at 12C (at 2000m altitude).
Motor (Red): Despite the reduced power and cold ambient air, the motor temperature does not stabilize. It continues to climb steadily from 92C to 110C, indicating it cannot dissipate the heat it's generating.
Battery (Green): Stabilizes at a high temperature of 53-54C.
3.Second Climb (Time 40 to 42 minutes):
Ambient (Light Blue): Drops again from 12C to 5C as the UAV climbs to 3000m.
Motor (Red): Spikes violently in response to the high power draw, jumping from 110C to a critical peak 138C. 
Battery (Green): Jumps from 54C to 66-67C.
4.Second Cruise (Time 42 to 80 minutes):
Ambient (Light Blue): Stays constant at 5C.
Motor (Red): Begins to cool very slowly, reaching 125C by the 80min mark, but remains at an extremely high temperature.
Battery (Green): Remains quite stable at its new, decreasing only 2-3C
5.Descent (Time 80 minutes onwards):
Ambient (Light Blue): Rises as the UAV descends.
Motor (Red) & Battery (Green): With propulsion power cut, both components finally begin to cool down at a much faster rate.
